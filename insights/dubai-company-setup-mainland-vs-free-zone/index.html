Setting up a company in Dubai: mainland or free zone?

The first decision for any new company in Dubai is where to license it. That choice shapes who you can sell to, what office you need, how many visas you get and how your profits are taxed. Here is how the two routes compare today.

Updated 27 November 2026 · 8 min read

The short answer

Choose a mainland company if your customers are in the UAE: retail, services to local businesses and residents, construction, or government work. Choose a free zone company if you mainly trade internationally, hold investments, need a fast start, or work in a sector served by a specialist zone such as commodities, media or technology.

The gap between the two has narrowed. Mainland companies can now be fully foreign-owned for most activities, and since 2025 Dubai free zone companies can apply for a permit to work on the mainland. The decision is now less about ownership and more about customers and tax position.

Mainland companies

A mainland company is licensed by Dubai's Department of Economy and Tourism (DET). Since the 2021 amendments to the Commercial Companies Law, most commercial and industrial activities can be 100% foreign-owned without an Emirati partner. A limited list of strategic activities still has conditions.

  • You can trade with anyone in the UAE, open branches in other emirates and bid for government contracts.
  • You need a registered office with an Ejari tenancy contract. The number of employee visas generally depends on office size.
  • You can add activities to the licence as the business grows.
  • Banks often find mainland companies straightforward to onboard, because the business is clearly anchored in the UAE.

Free zone companies

The UAE has dozens of free zones, each with its own authority, rules and requirements. Dubai's best known include DMCC, Dubai Silicon Oasis, Meydan, IFZA and Dubai South. DIFC is a separate financial free zone with its own courts and regulator.

  • Set-up is quick and largely online, often within a week, with packages that bundle the licence, a flexible desk and a set number of visas.
  • Each zone licenses a defined list of activities, so check that yours is on it.
  • Free zones offer customs benefits for goods and, for qualifying companies, a 0% corporate tax rate on qualifying income.
  • Selling directly to mainland customers has traditionally required a distributor, a mainland branch or, now, the new DET permit.

New: free zone companies on the mainland

Dubai Executive Council Resolution No. 11 of 2025 allows non-financial Dubai free zone companies to operate on the mainland. DET launched the permit in October 2025. There are two routes: a branch licence, valid for one year and renewable, and a temporary permit for specific activities of up to six months. The activity must appear on DET's approved list, and financial firms in DIFC are excluded.

This makes a free zone company more flexible than before, but it adds a second licence and separate accounting for mainland work. Mainland income can also affect a free zone company's tax position, as explained below.

Corporate tax and VAT

UAE corporate tax applies to both routes. The standard rate is 9% on taxable profit above AED 375,000; profit up to that amount is taxed at 0%.

  • Qualifying Free Zone Person: a free zone company can pay 0% on qualifying income if it keeps adequate substance in the zone, earns qualifying income, follows transfer pricing rules, prepares audited financial statements and does not elect into the standard regime. Status is tested every year.
  • De minimis limit: non-qualifying income must stay below 5% of total revenue or AED 5 million, whichever is lower. Income from mainland customers is in most cases non-qualifying. Breaching the conditions means 9% on all taxable income for that year and the following four years.
  • Small Business Relief: companies with revenue of AED 3 million or less can elect to be treated as having no taxable income. In August 2026 the Ministry of Finance extended the relief to tax periods ending on or before 31 December 2029. It is not available to Qualifying Free Zone Persons.
  • Every company must register for corporate tax with the Federal Tax Authority, and file a return each year, even when no tax is due.
  • VAT at 5% applies to both routes. Registration is mandatory once taxable supplies exceed AED 375,000 in 12 months.

The steps to a licence

  1. Define the activity, the customers and where the revenue will come from. This drives everything else.
  2. Choose the jurisdiction and legal form: mainland LLC or sole establishment, or a free zone company (FZ-LLC or FZE), or a branch of a foreign company.
  3. Reserve the trade name and obtain initial approval from DET or the free zone authority.
  4. Prepare and sign the memorandum of association and shareholder documents. Foreign corporate shareholders need attested documents.
  5. Secure the office: an Ejari-registered lease on the mainland, or a package desk or office in the free zone.
  6. Receive the trade licence.
  7. Obtain the establishment card, then residence visas and Emirates IDs for shareholders and staff.
  8. Open the corporate bank account. Expect detailed questions on ownership, source of funds and expected transactions.
  9. Register for corporate tax and, where needed, VAT, and set up bookkeeping from day one.

Mistakes we see most often

  • Choosing a free zone package in a hurry, then discovering the activity or the customers need a mainland licence.
  • Assuming free zone means 0% tax. The 0% rate depends on meeting the qualifying conditions every year.
  • Underestimating banking. Account opening is often the slowest step, so prepare a clear business plan and ownership file early.

Frequently asked questions

Can a foreigner own 100% of a Dubai mainland company?

Yes, for most commercial and industrial activities. Since 2021 an Emirati partner is no longer required, although a short list of strategic activities still has ownership conditions.

Is a free zone company tax-free?

Not automatically. A Qualifying Free Zone Person pays 0% on qualifying income, but must meet substance, audit, transfer pricing and de minimis conditions every year. Other income is taxed at 9% above AED 375,000.

Can a Dubai free zone company work with mainland clients?

Yes. Since October 2025 a non-financial Dubai free zone company can apply to DET for a branch licence or a temporary permit for approved activities. Check the effect on its corporate tax position first.

How long does it take to set up a company in Dubai?

A free zone licence can be issued in a few working days. A mainland licence usually takes one to four weeks. Visas and the bank account add time, so plan for one to three months until the company is fully operational.

Do I need an office?

A mainland company needs an Ejari-registered office. Free zone packages usually include a flexible desk, which is enough for the licence, although larger visa needs require more space.
