The Stonewynn view

A gas-led build-out, financed off balance sheet

The UAE is in the middle of its largest energy construction cycle since the 2000s, and the centre of gravity has shifted decisively from oil to gas. Of the projects we track, the great majority of disclosed value sits in gas processing, sour-gas development, LNG export and the pipelines that connect them. The logic is simple: domestic gas self-sufficiency by 2030, Ruwais LNG's first cargo in 2028, and a chemicals hub at Ruwais that needs feedstock.

Three patterns matter for anyone looking to participate. First, ADNOC is packaging megaprojects into multiple EPC lots and financing them with structured debt, which spreads risk but rewards contractors and suppliers who can work inside a joint-venture consortium. Second, Chinese engineering groups now sit alongside the European and Korean incumbents on flagship awards, changing the supply-chain map. Third, in-country value requirements mean fabrication, logistics, accommodation, facilities management and local subcontracting are where much of the money actually lands.

The risks are equally clear: fabrication-yard and marine-installation capacity is close to fully booked into the late 2020s, one major contractor is working through administration, and every Ruwais LNG feed project sits on the same 2028 critical path.

Where the value is

Gas processing, sour gas and LNG account for the bulk of the disclosed pipeline. Offshore oil (Upper and Lower Zakum) adds a further $17bn-plus.

Who is winning the work

NMDC Energy, McDermott, Saipem, Technip Energies, Tecnimont, Wison and CPECC hold the largest packages; Wood, Petrofac and Kent lead the EPCM programmes.

What to watch next

The Bab gas cap EPC tenders, Borouge's EU2 cracker award, and whether Ruwais LNG's feed infrastructure holds its 2028 schedule.
